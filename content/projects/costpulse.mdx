## The problem

AWS spend is invisible until the monthly bill arrives, and checking it means giving people console billing access most of the team shouldn't have.

## What it does

Type `/cost` in an allowed Slack channel and CostPulse replies with spend by service:

- `/cost` or `/cost month`: month to date (on the 1st, the full previous month)
- `/cost today`: today so far, in UTC
- `/cost week`: the last 7 days

The reply lists the top 5 services plus an "Other" row, and the total always equals the sum of the displayed rows.

## Architecture

- **One Lambda behind a Function URL**, deployed with AWS SAM to `us-east-1`.
- **Bolt lazy listeners** acknowledge Slack inside its 3-second limit, then the function re-invokes itself asynchronously to query Cost Explorer and post the in-channel reply through `response_url`.
- **Secrets** (signing secret, bot token) live in SSM Parameter Store as `SecureString`, read once per cold start.
- **In-memory cache** per date range for one hour, so repeated commands don't pay for repeated Cost Explorer calls.

## Least privilege, on both sides

- **Slack:** exactly one bot scope, `commands`. Replies go back through `response_url`, so the app needs no permission to post, read messages or see users.
- **AWS:** an explicit IAM role with five actions: `ce:GetCostAndUsage`, `ssm:GetParameter` on two parameters, a self-invoke for the lazy listener, and writes to its own log group. Tests fail if the policy ever widens.
- **Channel allowlist:** commands from any other channel get a private hint, and an empty allowlist rejects everything.

## Cost guardrails

A public Function URL has no rate limiting, and Lambda bills even rejected requests. So the stack adds a $5 monthly account budget with email alerts, a CloudWatch alarm on more than 1,000 invocations in 5 minutes, and a documented kill switch (reserved concurrency 0) that was tested live.

## Results

- 55 tests, including signed-request tests that drive Bolt's real Lambda adapter and replay the self-invocation.
- Warm acknowledgements in about 120 ms; a cold start of about 3 seconds on the server, which is the next thing to tune.
